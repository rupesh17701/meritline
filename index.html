Demand reference is the FY 2025–26 all-time peak (270.7 GW) applied as a stress test to both scenarios, not a claim about the actual hour it occurred. The IEX price is a thin, residual marginal market — not the whole system's average cost — which is why it can print above what this stack alone implies.

Analyst Notes

Reading the numbers
  • Evening peak is where the market breaks. Today's live DAM shows purchase bids running roughly 2× sell bids after sunset, with prices brushing the ₹10/kWh ceiling — solar drops off before demand peaks, and dispatchable capacity is thin exactly when it's needed most.
  • Capacity is keeping pace; flexibility isn't. Energy shortage was just 0.03% nationally in FY 2025–26 despite record demand, but gas remains under 4% of installed capacity and battery storage is still mostly under construction — the physical-adequacy story is fine, the flexibility story isn't.
  • The grid still leans on long transmission corridors. The Western region carries the largest installed base (173 GW) while the Northern region set the highest regional peak-demand record (91.2 GW) — synchronized heat events will keep testing inter-regional transfer capacity.
  • Intraday is pricing calmer than day-ahead. July's RTM average (₹4.41/kWh) sat below DAM (₹4.99/kWh), a spread consistent with better same-day renewable forecasting — a live signal for storage arbitrage and demand-response strategy.
